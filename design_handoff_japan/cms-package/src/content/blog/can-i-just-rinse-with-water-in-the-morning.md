Many people believe that washing only with water in the morning is good for skin health. After all, we double-cleanse in the evening to remove makeup and fine dust, so the logic is that we should minimize irritation to the skin in the morning. I, too, followed a dermatologist's advice and washed only with water in the morning for six months. It was effective at the time, but once my skin recovered and its barrier function returned to normal, I realized that rinsing with water alone was not enough. It even had the counterproductive effect of clogging my pores.

So, is it really okay to skip morning cleansing? Pharmacist Marta Masi emphasizes the importance of washing with water and a cleanser every morning. It's easy to assume your skin is clean in the morning because you cleansed thoroughly before bed, but more than you think accumulates on your skin overnight.

## What Happens to Your Skin While You Sleep

Skin cleansing expert Cristina Galmiche explains, "Just because you washed your skin clean before going to bed does not mean it stays perfectly clean until the morning." During sleep, impurities, residual skincare products, and sweat accumulate on the skin. Although invisible to the eye, these residues can clog pores and cause oxidation.

## The Importance of Morning Cleansing

- Removes sweat, sebum, and skincare residues.
- Prevents blackheads and acne.
- Supplies oxygen to the skin and activates micro-blood circulation.
- Improves skin texture and radiance.
- Enhances the effectiveness of serums and creams.
- Strengthens the skin barrier.

While it is important to reduce irritation when your skin is sensitive or the barrier is damaged, you must remove the sebum and residues accumulated overnight once your skin condition has recovered. Active ingredients can only work properly if you apply serums or creams to a cleanly prepped surface. Galmiche notes, "Morning cleansing is an essential habit that makes the skin brighter and fresher, preparing it to receive the next set of products."

When you cleanse properly in the morning, you remove the sweat, sebum, and hair product residues that built up overnight. It helps prevent blackheads and breakouts, and refines skin texture to bring out a natural glow. If you use products containing active ingredients like Vitamin C, glycolic acid, or hyaluronic acid, you will see even greater effects on skin that has been properly cleaned and prepared.

## How to Cleanse in the Morning

- Choose the right cleanser: Not every skin type should be cleansed in the same way. It is important to choose a cleanser based on your skin type. For oily or combination skin with excess sebum, a gel or foam cleanser with niacinamide or salicylic acid is suitable. Conversely, for dry or sensitive skin, a gentle, sulfate-free milk or cream cleanser is better.
- Apply with your hands: The method of cleansing is also important. Rather than rubbing with a cotton pad, it is better to gently massage your face with your hands. Massaging in circular motions for about 30 seconds helps remove surface impurities while promoting blood circulation.
- Rinse with lukewarm water: Water that is too hot can damage the lipids that make up the skin barrier, and water that is too cold may not sufficiently remove cleanser residue, so it is best to use lukewarm water.
- Press, don't rub, with a towel: When drying your face at the end, gently press to remove moisture instead of rubbing. It is also important to use a clean towel and change it frequently.

Morning cleansing should be flexible. You need to observe your current skin condition and cleanse as necessary. If your routine of washing only with water is working well for you right now, feel free to keep it; however, if your skin condition has changed, it is time to try a new approach. One small habit can change your skin's condition.
